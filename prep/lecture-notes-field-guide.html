Say this in 30 seconds

Lecture Notes is an Android app that turns a recorded lecture into study material. It transcribes the audio on the phone with Whisper, so the recording never leaves the device. It can label who is speaking, then sends only the text to an AI service the student chooses, such as free Groq, to write structured notes, flashcards and a quiz. Those feed a spaced-repetition study mode. It's built with Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, Room, WorkManager, and native C++ through JNI. It's open source, and anyone can install it from its website.

If you're asked who wrote the code: be straightforward. The app was built with an AI coding assistant working under your direction. You set the requirements, made the product decisions (free providers first, a concise-notes limit, speaker labels with a merge tool, dropping on-device notes), tested every feature on your own phone with a real 80-minute lecture, and reported the problems that got fixed. Interviewers respect that answer far more than a claim that falls apart under a follow-up question.

[02:00] How it works end to end

The pipeline

Every lecture follows the same path. Each heavy step runs in the background with WorkManager, saves its progress to the database, and can resume after the app is killed.

Capture

A foreground service records 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM straight into a WAV file, which is exactly what Whisper expects, so no conversion is needed later. A foreground service (one with a visible notification) keeps a 3-hour lecture going with the screen off. Imports decode any format with Android's MediaCodec and resample to 16 kHz. For videos, only the audio track is copied out, without re-encoding.

RecorderService.ktWav.ktImporter.ktAudioDecoder.ktResampler.ktAudioExtractor.kt

Transcribe on the phone

The audio is split into ~2-minute chunks at quiet moments. Each chunk goes through whisper.cpp, a C++ port of OpenAI's Whisper, called from Kotlin through JNI. Segments (start time, end time, text) are saved after every chunk, which makes each chunk a checkpoint. The thread count matches the fast CPU cores the app is currently allowed to use. Heavy work pauses when the phone gets too hot and waits while the battery is low.

ProcessWorker.ktChunkPlanner.ktWhisperNative.ktwhisper_jni.cppThermal.ktHeavyWork.kt

Pick the right model for the phone

The first time Speech models is opened, the app scans the hardware (RAM, chip, fast cores, free storage) and runs a speed test by itself: Tiny transcribes a bundled 30-second clip, and each model's measured cost ratio turns that into minutes per lecture hour. It recommends the most accurate model that handles a 1-hour lecture in 20 minutes or less. Models too big for the phone's RAM or storage are blocked with the reason, based on measured peak memory. Models download with resume support and are checked against a SHA-256 hash.

DeviceCheck.ktDeviceInfo.ktWhisperModels.ktModelDownloadWorker.ktbench30.wav

Label speakers (optional)

sherpa-onnx finds speech turns and turns each voice into an embedding, a list of numbers that describes how the voice sounds. The lecture is processed in 10-minute pieces to save memory, and voices are linked across pieces by cosine similarity. Each transcript line gets the speaker it overlaps most. The user can listen to samples, rename speakers, and merge two speakers who are really the same person.

Diarizer.ktSpeakerLinker.ktSpeakerWorker.ktSpeakerEdits.kt

Read slides and PDFs

Attached PDFs, PowerPoints, photos and text files are turned into text on the phone: the PDF text layer through PdfBox, scanned pages and photos through ML Kit OCR, and PowerPoint slides by reading their XML. A lecture can even be made from slides alone, with no recording.

TextExtractor.ktAttachmentWorker.ktDocumentImporter.ktSlidePicker.kt

Write notes with AI

Only text is sent, with timestamps, plus the most relevant slides and speaker tags. If the whole lecture fits the model's limit it goes in one request. Otherwise it's split into parts and merged (map-reduce). The app learns each provider's real limits from its error messages, waits out rate limits, saves finished parts so a failed run can resume, repairs broken JSON, and enforces an optional word budget. The result is JSON with sections, explanations, key terms, class Q&A, flashcards and a quiz.

NotesWorker.ktNotesGenerator.ktPrompts.ktNotesLength.ktStudyNotes.ktOpenAiCompatibleClient.ktAnthropicClient.kt

Study, export, back up

Flashcards go into a review deck scheduled with SM-2, a spaced-repetition method: cards you know come back less and less often. The quiz records scores. Notes export as PDF, Markdown, or an Anki import file. A backup zip holds everything and restores onto a fresh install.

Scheduler.ktCardSync.ktStudyScreen.ktQuizScreen.ktNotesExport.ktBackup.kt
[04:00] What we did, in order

Timeline

Sixteen commits over two days, 5–6 October 2026, then device-check improvements on 26 October and a public release with its own website on 27 October. Each one was built, installed on a real phone (a vivo I2405 with a Snapdragon 8s Gen 4) and tried on a real lecture before moving on.

a1e6a0b5 Oct

Foundation: record, import, transcribe, notes

Recording service, audio and video import, whisper.cpp through JNI, chunked background transcription, subjects to organise lectures, and the first AI notes through Groq with your own key. Per-subject prompts (template, extra instructions, or a fully custom prompt) came from your question about different prompts per category.

21ea3e65 Oct

Concise notes

You said the notes were stretching out too long. The prompt was tightened ("comprehensive but not verbose"), the prompt size is now measured instead of guessed, and stray timestamps inside bullet text are removed.

56099145 Oct

Study mode

A lecture picker (your request: choose which lectures to study), SM-2 flashcards, a quiz with saved scores, a cram mode for exam revision, and search inside notes.

c47a03e5 Oct

Slides and PDFs

On-device text extraction and OCR. Slides-only lectures need no recording. Notes cite "Slide N", and the most relevant slides are sent alongside each part of the transcript.

1b9d55f6 Oct

Device check

An on-phone speed test and a model recommendation based on measured speed rather than guesses from RAM alone.

1af89426 Oct

Speaker labels

sherpa-onnx diarization in 10-minute pieces, speakers linked across pieces, and a Class Q&A section in the notes.

22a7558 · 55f2e386 Oct

Speaker fixes

The default threshold split the lecturer into 15 speakers. Raising it to 0.9 gave 8. A plain warning that labels can be wrong, plus listen, rename and merge tools, handle the rest. The speaker panel no longer closes while a voice sample plays.

90fe4816 Oct

Polish (M8)

Release build (arm64 only, R8, signed), pauses when the phone overheats, battery safeguards, AAC compression after transcription (~115 → ~14 MB per hour), PDF, Markdown and Anki exports, and search inside slides. The on-device notes milestone (M6) was dropped as impractical on a phone.

a8b7bb2 · 113181c6 Oct

Length limit and counts

A word budget stated in the prompt and enforced afterwards, with a "No limit" mode. You choose how many flashcards and quiz questions to get.

0989d026 Oct

Bad-JSON recovery

Groq sometimes rejects the model's own reply as invalid JSON. The app now repairs it, retries with a reminder, and finally retries without JSON mode.

ab2ac9d6 Oct

Transcript editing

Find and replace across the transcript, used to fix "ASM" → "agent". The speaker merge combined the lecturer's split voice.

591f4046 Oct

Anki and lecturer fixes

Testing with Anki's own importer found that quote marks merged cards and a leading "#" dropped them. Fields are now quoted. The AI is now told who the lecturer is, whatever that speaker is named.

c4919e1 · dcb9bff26 Oct

Ready for other phones

Your question about installing the app on someone else's phone exposed gaps. The speed test now runs automatically on the first visit. A phone that can't run any model gets an explanation instead of a Tiny recommendation. Models too big for the phone are blocked, with RAM limits backed by measured peak memory (Tiny 104 MB to Medium 843 MB). Tested on your phone: the automatic test recommended Small at ≈ 14 min per lecture hour.

6eb4e3b · 94c53946 Oct

Backup & restore, then the release switch

A one-file backup with a safe restore, tested by restoring your real backup into a separate test copy of the app. That test found that restored notes were hidden until an API key was entered, which was then fixed. Your phone was then moved from the debug build to the release build with no data lost.

019a4c2 · 57cbca727 Oct

Public release and website

The code went to a public GitHub repository (Dasa108/lecture-notes) after checking the whole history for keys. A one-page website in docs/ is served by GitHub Pages at dasa108.github.io/lecture-notes, and the signed APK is attached to the v0.1.0 GitHub Release, which the site's Download button links to. The site was then redesigned and checked at desktop and phone widths.

6b9416527 Oct

Release key safety

With strangers now installing the app, you asked whether the release key was set up right. The published APK was confirmed to be signed with the release key (certificate SHA-256 87509efe…037277). Two gaps were found and closed: release builds silently fell back to the debug key when keystore.properties was missing, and the key existed only on this computer. The build now refuses, and the key is backed up encrypted with gpg.
